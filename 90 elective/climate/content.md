## Climate impact of large models

The relationship between AI and climate change is not the most prominent one in current debates regarding AI, but very large models can actually emit a significant amount of CO2 with all of the power they consume during development and training. Exact estimates for this are notoriously difficult to obtain, as these usually requires many assumptions; from parameters used in training, to the exact hardware on which the models were trained, and even the mix of different energy source (wind/solar/gas/coal/etc.) in the local power grid (Lacoste et al., 2019).

However, in recent years some rough estimate have been made for larger models. For example, Patterson et al. (2021) estimate GPT-3 emitted 552.1 tons of CO2 during training, which is roughly equivalent to 400 flights from Schiphol to LAX. These are only the emissions for a single training run, while the model was probably tweaked and retrained several times during development. In addition, GPT-3 is actually a smaller predecessor to the more recent ChatGPT and GPT-4 models, so those emission numbers will probably have been even higher still.

As a result, according to Dhar (2020) the role of AI in the climate crisis seems destined to be a ‘dual’ one: “On the one hand, it can help reduce the effects of the climate crisis, such as in smart grid design, developing low-emission infrastructure, and modelling climate change predictions. On the other hand, AI is itself a significant emitter of carbon”. Given the impact of even training a single large model, this assignment focuses on possible regulations to mitigate the climate cost of AI-models, including options of weighing these costs against their proposed benefits.

Read the following paper by Philip Hacker on *“Sustainable AI regulation”*, where he puts forward several suggestions on how to deal, at a regulatory level, with the dual role of AI in combatting climate change: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00292)

This paper contains the following proposals for regulation:

1. Co-regulation (page 21-22);
2. Sustainability impact assessments (page 23-26);
3. Restrictions on training AI models (page 26);
4. An emissions trading regime for AI (page 26-27, page 7 for EU ETS);
5. Consumption caps based on an AI’s model’s social utility (page 27-28).

Next, consider the implementation of each of these proposals. To what extent are these realistic to implement and therefore likely to make AI more environmentally sustainable?

For this writing assignment you should argue which of these suggestions for regulation you think would be the most promising one to actually implement, and why. In addition, mention what open challenges you think there would still be for implementation and how they might be addressed.

Integrate your answer into a coherent text, containing around 500 words. The minimum requirement for the assignment is 350 words.
